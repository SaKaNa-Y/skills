# Discovery entrypoints

Choose an entrypoint that matches a project clue, then trace it to an additional user outcome. The main workflow owns evidence, existing alternatives, feasibility, and prior-work checks.

## Missing context

When users inspect a result, identify the background needed to understand or judge it. Follow where that background lives and how users retrieve it. Consider whether bringing it into the task would improve their understanding or decision, and when it would add distraction.

## Hidden information dimensions

When grouping, filtering, or summarizing helps one task, inspect what other decision-relevant distinctions it hides. Follow a concrete case where users need both dimensions. Compare preserving the existing organization with adding another way to recognize or act on the hidden distinction.

## Lost meaning in presentation

When content is available but difficult to interpret, inspect what its structure or semantics could communicate. Compare the current representation with one that helps the user's task. Existing renderer support is a feasibility clue; additional understanding supplies the value. Account for fallback and delivery costs when they can change the choice.

## Information needed together

When a task requires comparing or discussing multiple objects, observe whether users can refer to them together. Follow switching, obstruction, or reconstruction caused by their separation. Consider the actual content and available workspace before choosing a layout or another way to retain context.

## Preparation before action

When users must load, convert, configure, or otherwise prepare something before their intended action, locate the prerequisite and its owner. Ask whether the action can handle that preparation while preserving meaningful control over cost, timing, or side effects. Distinguish an additional supported path from a repair of an existing promise.

## Existing user environments

When users already have a tool, account, data source, or local capability relevant to the task, inspect whether the project can use it. Identify the outcome this connection enables and the assumptions that differ from supported integrations. Available adapters or APIs establish a possible path, not demand by themselves.

For manual transfers between adjacent tasks and gaps across content providers or consumers, use the discovery routes in the main workflow.
